Chase checklist

Print this and work down the list. Keep every step in writing.

  1. Check the invoice terms, the due date you agreed, and that your customer is a business or a public authority, not a consumer.
  2. Confirm the invoice was received and that the bank details on it are right.
  3. On day 1 overdue, send a short, polite reminder with the invoice attached.
  4. On day 7, send a firmer reminder and ask for the date of the next payment run.
  5. Phone the person who approves payment, then email the same day to record what you agreed.
  6. If it is still unpaid, send a statutory interest notice saying you may add interest and the fixed compensation.
  7. Use the figures above and send a separate invoice for the interest and compensation. Do not quietly change the original invoice.
  8. Send a final letter before action with a clear deadline and the total you will claim. Only name a step you are prepared to take.
  9. Keep every reminder on one email thread so you have a dated record.
  10. If the deadline passes, consider Money Claim Online for a debt in England and Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own court processes.

Questions

How does this late payment calculator work?

It works out statutory interest on a late UK business-to-business invoice under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. Interest is the invoice amount multiplied by the reference rate you enter plus 8%, divided by 365, then multiplied by the number of days late. It also adds the fixed compensation: £40 under £1,000, £70 from £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 at £10,000 or more, once per invoice. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

What Bank of England rate should I enter?

The field is prefilled at 3.75%, the reference rate for debts that became late between 1 July and 31 December 2026. Statutory interest is 8% plus that reference rate, which is 11.75% a year. The reference rate is the Bank of England base rate on 30 June or 31 December. The current Bank Rate (4% since 5 November 2026) becomes the reference rate from 1 January 2027 if it is still in force on 31 December. Check bankofengland.co.uk before you rely on it.

Can I use this for an invoice to a consumer?

No. Statutory interest and the fixed sum apply to qualifying business-to-business debts, including invoices to public authorities. They do not apply to consumers, and a contract may already set a different late-payment rate.
