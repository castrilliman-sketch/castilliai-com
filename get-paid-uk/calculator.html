How UK late payment interest is worked out

For a qualifying business-to-business debt, statutory interest is 8% a year above the Bank of England reference rate. The calculation is the one GOV.UK shows:

  1. Amount owed × statutory rate = annual interest
  2. Annual interest ÷ 365 = daily interest
  3. Daily interest × days late = interest to claim (it starts the day after the due date)

Then add the fixed sum for recovery costs: £40 (debt under £1,000), £70 (£1,000 to £9,999.99) or £100 (£10,000 or more), once per invoice.

Worked example: a £2,400 invoice due 15 September 2026, still unpaid on 14 November 2026 (60 days). Reference rate 3.75% + 8% = 11.75%. Annual interest £282.00, about 77p a day, so £46.36 for 60 days, plus the £70 fixed sum: £116.36 on top of the £2,400.

If no payment date was agreed, a B2B payment is late 30 days after the customer received the invoice or you delivered, whichever is later. Send a new invoice for the interest and fixed sum rather than changing the original.

Questions

How is late payment interest calculated in the UK?

For a qualifying business-to-business debt, statutory interest is 8% a year above the Bank of England reference rate. Multiply the amount owed by that rate to get annual interest, divide by 365 for daily interest, then multiply by the number of days late. Interest starts the day after the due date.

What rate does the calculator use?

By default it uses the statutory reference rate: the Bank Rate in force on 30 June (for interest starting 1 July to 31 December) or 31 December (for interest starting 1 January to 30 June). For July to December 2026 that is 3.75%, giving 11.75% a year. You can also choose the current Bank Rate (4% from 5 November 2026) or enter your own.

What is the £40, £70 or £100 fee?

It is a fixed sum for recovery costs under section 5A of the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998: £40 for a debt under £1,000, £70 for £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 for £10,000 or more, once per invoice.

Can I use this for an invoice to a consumer?

No. Statutory interest and the fixed sums only apply when both you and your customer are acting in the course of a business, including public authorities.
