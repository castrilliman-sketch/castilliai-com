Statutory interest and fixed-fee calculator

For late business-to-business invoices in the UK, under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998. Information only — not legal advice.

B2B only. Statutory interest and the fixed recovery fee do not apply if your customer is a consumer (a private individual buying for personal use). They also may not apply if your contract already sets a different late-payment interest rate, except that you cannot agree a lower rate with a public authority. Invoice details

Statutory interest is 8 percentage points above the official Bank of England Bank Rate for qualifying business-to-business debts. The official Bank Rate is 4% from 5 November 2026, following the Monetary Policy Committee meeting ending 4 November 2026. Source: bankofengland.co.uk and the Bank Rate history. Always check for a later decision before you invoice for interest.

The law uses a six-monthly reference rate: the Bank Rate in force on 31 December (for interest that starts between 1 January and 30 June) or on 30 June (for interest that starts between 1 July and 31 December). See the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Rate of Interest) (No. 3) Order 2002. This calculator uses that method by default. For invoices becoming overdue in July–December 2026, the reference rate is 3.75% (the rate in force on 30 June 2026), so the statutory rate is 11.75%. Daily interest follows the GOV.UK method: annual interest ÷ 365.

Fixed recovery fees are £40 (debt below £1,000), £70 (£1,000 to £9,999.99) or £100 (£10,000 or more), once per invoice. Guidance: GOV.UK late commercial payments, interest and recovery costs.
