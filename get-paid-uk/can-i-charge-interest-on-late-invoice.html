Can I charge interest on a late invoice?

On business-to-business invoices in the UK, usually yes. Here’s who it applies to, the rate for 2026, the £40 / £70 / £100 fixed sum, and a worked example.

Short answer: yes, if your customer is a business or public authority. Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 you can usually claim statutory interest at 8% above the Bank of England reference rate, plus a fixed sum of £40, £70 or £100 per invoice. You can’t use it against consumers, and it doesn’t apply if your contract already sets a different late-payment rate (with a public authority you can’t agree a lower one).

Who it applies to

  • Yes: sole traders, freelancers, partnerships and companies supplying goods or services to another business or a public authority. “Business” includes a profession.
  • No: customers who are private individuals buying for personal use (consumers). Employment contracts aren’t covered either.
  • Contract says otherwise: if your contract sets its own late-payment interest rate, you can’t also claim statutory interest — GOV.UK says to use the contract rate.

What you can add

1. Statutory interest

The rate is 8% a year above the Bank of England reference rate. The reference rate is not always today’s Bank Rate: the law uses the Bank Rate in force on 30 June for interest that starts between 1 July and 31 December, and on 31 December for interest that starts between 1 January and 30 June.

GOV.UK’s own worked example simply uses “the Bank of England base rate”; the free calculator lets you use either method.

2. A fixed sum for recovery costs

Amount of the debtFixed sum
Up to £999.99£40
£1,000 to £9,999.99£70
£10,000 or more£100

You can charge it once per invoice, once statutory interest starts to run. If your reasonable recovery costs are higher, section 5A(2A) of the Act lets you claim the difference too.

Worked example

A sole trader invoices a local company £800, due 1 October 2026. It is still unpaid on 14 November 2026 — 44 days late.

Statutory rate11.75% (reference rate 3.75% + 8%)
Annual interest£800 × 11.75% = £94.00
Daily interest£94.00 ÷ 365 = about 26p
Interest for 44 days£11.33
Fixed sum (debt under £1,000)£40
Total you may claim£851.33 (£800 + £11.33 + £40)

On a small invoice, the fixed sum is often worth more than the interest. Check your invoice in the free calculator.

How to claim it

  1. Confirm the invoice is late and the customer is a business or public authority.
  2. Work out the interest and fixed sum (the calculator shows a printable breakdown).
  3. Send a new invoice for the interest and fixed sum, referencing the original — GOV.UK recommends this rather than changing the original invoice.
  4. Keep chasing in writing. See how to chase an unpaid invoice for a schedule and wording.

Many people put a line in their terms and on each invoice saying they reserve the right to claim statutory interest and compensation under the 1998 Act. That isn’t required for the Act to apply, but it sets expectations early.

Common questions

Can I charge interest on a late invoice in the UK?

Yes, if your customer is a business or public authority. The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 lets you claim statutory interest at 8% above the Bank of England reference rate, plus a fixed sum of £40, £70 or £100, unless your contract sets a different late-payment rate.

Can I charge late payment interest to a consumer?

Not under the 1998 Act. It only covers contracts where both sides are acting in the course of a business. Any late-payment charge to a consumer would depend on the terms they agreed and consumer law.

What is the statutory interest rate right now?

For interest that starts between 1 July and 31 December 2026, the reference rate is 3.75% (the Bank Rate in force on 30 June 2026), so the statutory rate is 11.75% a year. Bank Rate rose to 4% on 5 November 2026; for interest starting in early 2027, use the Bank Rate in force on 31 December 2026 plus 8%.

How much is the fixed late payment fee?

£40 for a debt up to £999.99, £70 for £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 for £10,000 or more, once per invoice. If your reasonable recovery costs are higher, you can claim the difference.

Do I need a late payment clause in my contract?

No. Statutory interest is implied into qualifying business contracts by the Act. But if your contract sets its own late-payment rate, that rate applies instead.
