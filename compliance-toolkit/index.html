Why now.

New return, new data.

From 1 January 2027 the FCA is replacing five existing complaints returns with a single return, COM001. Firms should be collecting the new individual data points from that date, including two fields on customer vulnerability. The first reporting period is 1 January to 30 June 2027, with the first submissions from 1 July 2027. The log you use from 1 January matters.

The format may still change.

On 13 November 2026 the FCA withdrew its COM001 technical guides while it reviews the format, and said firms should not rely on the previous guides when developing reporting systems. We say so plainly. Full-toolkit buyers get a free update once the technical format is final.

Arrears records still have to be clear.

CONC 7 requires firms to treat customers in or approaching arrears or in default with forbearance and due consideration, taking account of the customer’s circumstances. A simple evidence pack helps you record what was offered, why, and what happened.

In PS25/19 the FCA estimated that around 22,200 small firms would be affected by the new complaints reporting process, out of about 23,600 firms in total. Many of those firms won’t have an in-house compliance team.

Which rules does it relate to?

Complaints handling under the FCA’s Dispute Resolution: Complaints sourcebook (DISP), including the acknowledgement, eight-week response, final response and summary resolution communication rules in DISP 1.5 and DISP 1.6. The log is designed around the new COM001 complaints return, which starts collecting data on 1 January 2027. The arrears pack relates to CONC 7 (Arrears, default and recovery), including the requirement to treat customers in or approaching arrears or in default with forbearance and due consideration. Always check the latest Handbook and guidance at fca.org.uk.
